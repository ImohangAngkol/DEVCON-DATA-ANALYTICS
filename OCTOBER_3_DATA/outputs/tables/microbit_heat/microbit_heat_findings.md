### Interpretation Limits

The assessment contains only five items. Results should be interpreted as
performance on those items rather than comprehensive evidence of practical
micro:bit proficiency.

Repeated-question familiarity and guessing may contribute to observed
changes. Without a comparison group, the program's causal effect cannot
be isolated.

## Recommendations and Next Steps

### Prioritize Remaining Assessment Gaps

Provide additional guided practice for **Decomposition; Abstraction**,
which had the lowest post-test correct-answer rate.

Use worked examples and short code-tracing activities in a heat-alert
context. Follow these with new questions to check whether participants
can apply the concepts beyond the original assessment item.

### Investigate Declining Items

The following items had lower post-test accuracy: Debugging: -12.0 percentage points. Review the original questions, answer options, and response patterns before attributing the declines to a cause.

Do not assume that a decline was caused by hardware difficulty,
frustration, or cognitive overload. Those explanations require
additional evidence from observations or participant feedback.

### Include Practical Assessment

Add a short task in which participants explain and test a heat-alert
program using supplied temperature values.

Evaluate the program's behavior and the participant's explanation.
This would provide evidence of practical application beyond
multiple-choice accuracy.

### Check Retention

Use a delayed follow-up assessment with additional questions per skill.
This would help examine retention and reduce reliance on a single item.

### Prediction Analysis

Post-test prediction will be explored separately using pre-test inputs.
Models will be compared with simple baselines using held-out predictions.
Observed score changes and prediction performance will be reported as
different findings.
